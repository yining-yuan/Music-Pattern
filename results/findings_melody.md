# What makes a chord-progression-with-melody liked — findings

_Generated by `discover_melody.py` (deterministic, `random_state=0`). Stimulus = 4 chords (16 notes) + a 10-note melody on top. Re-run the script to regenerate._

## TL;DR

- Screened **248 candidate single-feature patterns** (see `candidate_patterns.csv`), carried the most promising into a held-out confirmation test, and **6 of 16** survived Benjamini–Hochberg FDR (α=0.05) with a stable bootstrap CI.
- **Direction of the signal:** the surviving patterns all point the same way — a **higher, rising melody line** (its middle-to-late notes sitting high) together with an **upward inner-voice drift** leans **LIKE**; a low/sagging melody leans **DISLIKE**. Melody height, not chord dissonance, is the dominant driver once the tune is included.
- **Caveat — global vs per-pattern:** a multivariate like-vs-rest classifier only reaches CV-AUC **0.55** vs null 0.50 (p = 0.161), i.e. **not** significant as a whole-item predictor. The confirmed patterns are real *univariate mean shifts* on held-out data (permutation + FDR), but they do not add up to a strong per-item classifier — treat them as genuine tendencies, not a predictor.
- **'Not sure' ordering:** dislike=-1.61, notsure=-1.13, like=-0.31 → notsure sits between dislike and like.
- Effect sizes per pattern are **small** (Cliff's δ typically 0.1–0.2): read these as *tendencies*, not a confident per-item predictor.

Near-duplicate stimuli (nearest neighbour in the full 26-note space) get *different* labels ~68% of the time. The engineered interval/melody features nonetheless extract signal above the raw-note ceiling (global CV-AUC 0.55, p=0.161).

## Method (one paragraph)

Data was split 60/40 into **discovery** and **held-out** sets. Every feature was first screened as a median split and dumped to `candidate_patterns.csv` (the exhaustive 'list of possible patterns'). Promising patterns were then *surfaced* on the discovery split only (Spearman monotonic screening, WRAcc subgroup discovery, contrast/emerging patterns, gradient-boosted importance) and *confirmed* on the held-out split with permutation tests, Cliff's δ effect sizes, and Benjamini–Hochberg FDR control — so no pattern is believed on the data that generated it. Survivors were bootstrapped for stability, and a k-NN label-disagreement estimate gives the noise ceiling. All features are pure arithmetic on the note integers (chord intervals/roughness/voice-leading plus melody shape and melody-vs-harmony fit); no music-theory labels were imposed.
